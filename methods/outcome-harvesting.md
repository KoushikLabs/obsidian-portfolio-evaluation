# Outcome Harvesting

## What it is

**Outcome Harvesting** is a method that works *backwards* from observed changes.
Instead of measuring progress against pre-set targets, it identifies **outcomes**
— defined as observable changes in the behaviour, relationships, actions,
activities, policies, or practices of individuals, groups, or organisations —
and then determines whether and how an intervention **contributed** to each one.

Developed by Ricardo Wilson-Grau and colleagues, it is especially suited to
settings where outcomes cannot be predicted in advance: advocacy, influencing,
systems change, networks, and innovation — exactly the kinds of "fuzzy" results
that fill funder portfolios.

## How it works (the cycle)

1. **Design the harvest** — agree the harvesting questions and what counts as an
   outcome with the users of the findings.
2. **Gather/draft outcome descriptions** — review documents and engage
   informants to draft outcomes: *what* changed, *who* changed, *when/where*, and
   the intervention's *contribution* to it.
3. **Engage informants/sources** to enrich and refine the outcome descriptions.
4. **Substantiate** — independent third parties verify a sample of outcomes
   (this is the credibility step).
5. **Analyse and interpret** — classify and synthesise outcomes against the
   harvesting questions.
6. **Support use** of findings.

## Why it matters for portfolio evaluation

- **Built for heterogeneity.** Because it doesn't require interventions to share
  indicators or targets, it can harvest outcomes across a *diverse* portfolio and
  then look for patterns — directly addressing the heterogeneity problem.
- **Contribution, not attribution.** Its claims are framed as plausible
  contribution, which is the realistic standard at portfolio scale (see
  [[concepts/aggregation-vs-attribution|aggregation vs attribution]]).
- **Aggregation route.** Harvested outcomes become the raw material for
  [[templates/cross-case-analysis-matrix|cross-case analysis]] and for an
  [[methods/evidence-gap-maps|Evidence Gap Map]].

## When to use it

- Portfolios of **advocacy / policy-influence** grants (a common funder pattern —
  see [[case-studies/T0029-brush-strokes-to-bold-strokes|T0029]]).
- Where outcomes are **emergent** and could not be pre-specified.
- Incubator cohorts where each venture's "win" looks different.

## Limitations / cautions

- Substantiation is essential — without it, harvested outcomes are just claims.
- Can over-collect positive outcomes; deliberately harvest negative/null ones too.
- Synthesis across many harvested outcomes still needs a disciplined
  cross-case method or it becomes anecdote soup.

## The portfolio being evaluated

A portfolio of **influencing / advocacy efforts** — a very common funder pattern
(funders rarely fund a single advocacy campaign; they fund a *portfolio* of
influence bets). The session is about drawing **portfolio-level lessons** out of
many individual influencing efforts.

## Methods used

A **"bricolage"** portfolio-evaluation approach — deliberately combining several
methods rather than relying on one:

- [[methods/outcome-harvesting|**Outcome Harvesting**]] — to identify the
  outcomes influencing efforts actually produced.
- [[methods/contribution-analysis|**Contribution Analysis**]] — to build
  credible contribution stories where attribution is impossible.
- [[methods/appreciative-inquiry|**Appreciative Inquiry**]] — to surface
  what worked and why, generatively.
- [[methods/rubrics|**Rubrics**]] — to structure evaluative judgements.
- **Quality-of-evidence assessments** — to weight evidence honestly.
- **Cross-case analysis** — to synthesise across the individual efforts into
  portfolio-level lessons.

This combination is the single best worked example in the knowledge base of the
[[methods/methods-index|methods]] folder being used *together*.

## Key challenge addressed

How to **draw out portfolio-level lessons from a set of diverse influencing
efforts** — where outcomes are emergent, attribution is impossible, and each
effort looks different. I.e. synthesis across heterogeneous, fuzzy cases
(open questions 2 and 3).

## Relevance to a funder / incubator

Very high and very transferable. Advocacy/influence portfolios are extremely
common for funders, and this "bricolage" (Outcome Harvesting + Contribution
Analysis + Appreciative Inquiry + rubrics + quality-of-evidence + cross-case
analysis) is a **ready-made recipe** for evaluating one. An incubator could apply
the same recipe to a cohort where each venture's "win" is different and emergent.

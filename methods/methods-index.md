# Methods

The building-block methods of portfolio-level evaluation. These are the
techniques that recur across the [[case-studies/case-studies-index|case studies]] and the
[[external-sources/external-sources-index|external guidance]]. Most real portfolio evaluations
**combine several** of them — Integrity calls this a *"bricolage"* approach (see
[[case-studies/T0029-brush-strokes-to-bold-strokes|T0029]]).

## Contents

| Method | One-line role in portfolio evaluation | Primary open question |
|---|---|---|
| [[methods/outcome-harvesting\|Outcome Harvesting]] | Identify what changed, then work backwards to the portfolio's contribution. | Q2, Q3 |
| [[methods/contribution-analysis\|Contribution Analysis]] | Build a defensible causal story of the portfolio's contribution amid other factors. | Q3 |
| [[methods/appreciative-inquiry\|Appreciative Inquiry]] | Surface what works and why, generatively, across diverse grantees. | Q2, Q4 |
| [[methods/rubrics\|Rubrics]] | Make "good" explicit so heterogeneous interventions can be judged against shared standards. | Q1, Q3 |
| [[methods/evidence-gap-maps\|Evidence Gap Maps]] | Map what the portfolio covers and where evidence is thin. | Q1, Q2 |
| [[methods/value-for-investment\|Value for Investment]] | Make explicit, reasoned judgements about whether value justifies resources. | Q3, Q4 |

(Open questions Q1–Q4 are defined in the [[index|README]].)

## Cross-cutting: synthesis methods

Several of the above are also *synthesis* methods — ways to combine many cases
into a portfolio-level picture. The recurring synthesis toolkit is:
**cross-case analysis**, **quality-of-evidence assessment**, and
**Evidence Gap Maps**. See the [[templates/cross-case-analysis-matrix|cross-case analysis matrix template]].

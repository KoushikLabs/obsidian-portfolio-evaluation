# Evidence Gap Maps (EGMs)

## What it is

An **Evidence Gap Map** is a structured, usually visual matrix that maps the
**existing evidence** on a topic against two dimensions — typically
**interventions** (rows) and **outcomes** (columns) — showing where evidence is
abundant, where it is thin, and where it is absent. Cells are populated with the
studies/evaluations that exist (often colour-/size-coded by volume and quality),
making **gaps** immediately visible.

EGMs come from the systematic-review tradition (3ie, Campbell Collaboration) but
are highly transferable to a funder's own portfolio.

## Why it matters for portfolio evaluation

- It is a **core portfolio-synthesis tool** (the conference billed
  [[case-studies/T0110-introduction-to-egms|T0110]] as an introduction to
  EGMs precisely for this reason).
- For a funder, an EGM built over the *portfolio itself* answers: **what does our
  portfolio actually cover?** Which intervention–outcome combinations are
  well-evidenced, which are under-evidenced bets, and where are we flying blind?
- It directly serves **open question 1** (criteria/coverage) and **open question
  2** (synthesis): it is a synthesis output *and* a planning tool that shows where
  the next evaluation effort or the next grant should go.
- It turns a heterogeneous pile of grants into a **single legible picture** — a
  powerful artefact for communicating to non-evaluator funders.

## Two ways funders/incubators use EGMs

1. **Evidence-of-effectiveness EGM** — map the *external* evidence base for the
   kinds of interventions you fund, to inform strategy and grant selection.
2. **Portfolio-coverage EGM** — map your *own* grants/ventures against your
   portfolio theory-of-change outcomes, to see coverage and evidence gaps within
   the portfolio.

## How to build one (quick recipe)

1. Define the framework: intervention categories (rows) × outcomes (columns),
   derived from the portfolio theory of change.
2. Populate cells with grants/studies/evaluations.
3. Code each by **volume** and **quality/confidence of evidence**.
4. Read off the gaps and over-concentrations; decide what they mean for strategy.

## Limitations / cautions

- A map of *what evidence exists*, not a verdict on *what works* — gaps may reflect
  under-study, not ineffectiveness.
- Quality coding requires a [[methods/rubrics|quality-of-evidence assessment]]; an EGM
  that ignores quality can mislead.

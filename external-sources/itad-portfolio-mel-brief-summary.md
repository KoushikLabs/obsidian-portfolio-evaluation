## 1. Definition of portfolio MEL used

Identical to the Norad brief: a portfolio is *"a collection of measures based on a
common underlying logic which are designed to contribute to achieving
higher-level objectives,"* and portfolio-level MEL *"involves gathering evidence
of results across the range of a portfolio's interventions, and conducting a
higher-level analysis"* of whether the portfolio's broader objectives are
achieved.

**Distinctive emphasis — "knowledge", not just "evidence".** The Itad framing
leans hard on a *broad* definition of what feeds MEL: monitoring data, evaluation
evidence, research, policy/grey literature, **and professional insight and
experience**. The deliberate word choice — **"knowledge" rather than "evidence"**
— signals that portfolio judgement must draw on more than formal studies. (The
Norad PDF operationalises this as the four knowledge types: professional,
operational, technical, contextual.)

## 2. Recommended portfolio-level evaluation questions

Same **strategic vs operational** split as the Norad brief, derived from the
portfolio theory of change. The Itad article's representative examples:

- **Operational:** *"how can we better support coordination among partners?"*
- **Strategic:** *"where else should we intervene in the system?"*

For the full formative/summative question matrix, see
[[external-sources/norad-portfolio-mel-guide-summary#2. Recommended portfolio-level evaluation questions|the Norad summary §2]].

## 3. How it handles aggregating across diverse interventions

**Distinctive emphasis — analytical judgement over arithmetic.** The Itad article
states the principle most crisply: *"making analytical judgements will give
richer, more robust insights into progress than simply aggregating numbers."*
This is the conceptual justification for the Norad brief's practical mechanism
("buckets" of indicators + periodic judgement + rubrics).

Shared with the Norad brief:
- **Contribution within a system**, not attribution.
- **Portfolio theory of change as the synthesis backbone** — integrating the work
  of multiple partnerships and situating their combined contribution in the
  broader system.
- **Build on existing programme-level data** and aggregate *up* (the Abt PMEL
  framing of "aggregate and additive value"), rather than re-measuring each
  intervention.

---

## 4. Frameworks / criteria recommended

Same as the Norad brief:
- **Portfolio-level theory of change** (primary framework; systems-oriented).
- **Theory-based evaluation** for whole-portfolio evaluations.
- **Contribution analysis** as the implied causal logic (Itad publish companion
  "Tools and Tips for Implementing Contribution Analysis").
- **Adaptive/knowledge-based management** — MEL embedded in the management cycle.
- **OECD-DAC criteria: not used** (consistent with the Norad PDF).

The **five implementation steps** are identical: (1) portfolio theory of change →
(2) strategic & operational questions → (3) monitoring processes → (4) plan
evaluations → (5) spaces for collective learning. See
[[external-sources/norad-portfolio-mel-guide-summary#5. The five-step model (the spine of the guide)|the Norad summary §5]].

---

## 5. What the Itad framing adds (net)

1. The **"knowledge not evidence"** reframing — a practical and political point for
   funders who over-privilege formal evaluation evidence.
2. The crisp principle that **analytical judgement beats number-aggregation** — the
   single best one-liner to quote to a funder anxious about "scoring" a portfolio.
3. An explicit link to **contribution analysis** as Itad's house method.

# Learning Plan — Advising Funders & Incubators on Portfolio Evaluation

A structured **8-week** self-study plan (a 6-week fast track is marked below) for
someone who wants to **advise funders and incubators on portfolio-level
evaluation**. It sequences the whole [[index|knowledge base]] from
**foundations → methods → synthesis & communication → embedding evaluation
culture**.

## Who this is for

You want to be the person a funder or incubator turns to when they ask: *"We've
got 30 grants / a cohort of 12 ventures — is the **portfolio** working, and what
should we change?"* By the end you should be able to **design, run the synthesis
for, and communicate** a portfolio evaluation, and help an organisation embed it.

## How to use this plan

- Each week has: a **learning objective**, **2–3 concepts/methods to study**, a
  **reflection question**, and a **small practical exercise**.
- Pick **one real (or realistic) funder/incubator** in Week 1 and carry it through
  every exercise. By Week 8 you'll have a complete worked portfolio evaluation
  framework for it.
- Keep your exercise outputs in a personal `/workbook` folder (create your own).
- Use the [[templates/prompts|reusable Claude prompts]] to draft and
  pressure-test — but **make the evaluative judgements yourself**.

## The through-line: four open questions

Everything maps back to the four open questions from the [[index|README]]:
**(Q1)** setting criteria across heterogeneous interventions; **(Q2)** synthesising
across cases; **(Q3)** defining success/credibility at portfolio scale; **(Q4)**
making findings actionable for funders. Each week, note which question you're
working on.

## Week 1 — Foundations: what portfolio evaluation is, and why funders need it

- **Learning objective:** Explain what portfolio-level evaluation is, why it
  differs from project evaluation, and why funders/incubators need it. Internalise
  the heterogeneity problem as the field's central tension. *(Q1, Q3)*
- **Study (concepts/methods):**
  - [[concepts/what-is-a-portfolio|concepts/what-is-a-portfolio]]
  - [[concepts/the-heterogeneity-problem|concepts/the-heterogeneity-problem]]
- **📚 Week 1 foundational readings (required):**
  - [[external-sources/norad-portfolio-mel-guide-summary|Norad — *A practical guide to MEL at a portfolio level*]]
    (read the structured summary, then the [PDF](https://www.norad.no/contentassets/edb6d72917a84b70bf1bed3283586efc/evaluation_brief_a-practical-guide-to-monitoring-evaluation-and-learning-at-a-portfolio-level.pdf)).
  - [[external-sources/itad-portfolio-mel-brief-summary|Itad — *Practical guidance for portfolio-level MEL*]]
    (the companion framing — note these two are the *same* underlying guidance).
- **Reflection question:** For your chosen funder/incubator, what would be lost if
  you only evaluated each grant/venture on its own terms and never at the
  portfolio level? What can *only* be seen at the aggregate?
- **Practical exercise:** Write a one-page **portfolio profile** of your chosen
  funder/incubator: what makes its grants/ventures a *portfolio* (not a list), and
  state, in your own words, how each of the four open questions shows up for it.

## Week 2 — The portfolio theory of change & setting evaluation questions

- **Learning objective:** Articulate a portfolio-level theory of change and derive
  evaluation questions at two distinct levels (project vs portfolio). *(Q1)*
- **Study (concepts/methods):**
  - [[concepts/portfolio-theory-of-change|concepts/portfolio-theory-of-change]]
  - [[methods/rubrics|methods/rubrics]] *(first pass — you'll deepen it in Week 4)*
  - The strategic/operational question matrix in the
    [[external-sources/norad-portfolio-mel-guide-summary#2. Recommended portfolio-level evaluation questions|Norad summary §2]].
- **Reflection question:** Where does a *shared* framework genuinely help your
  portfolio, and where would it impose ill-suited criteria? Would you use OECD-DAC
  at portfolio scale — and why or why not? (Contrast
  [[case-studies/T0302-portfoli-omg|T0302]] with the Norad guide.)
- **Practical exercise:** Draft a **portfolio theory of change** for your chosen
  funder (use [[templates/prompts|prompt #2]]), then build a **strategic ×
  operational / formative × summative question matrix** and circle the "vital few"
  questions you'd actually evaluate.

---

## Week 3 — Methods I: evidence of outcomes & contribution

- **Learning objective:** Use the core outcome-identification and causal-inference
  methods, and reason in terms of contribution rather than attribution. *(Q2, Q3)*
- **Study (concepts/methods):**
  - [[methods/outcome-harvesting|methods/outcome-harvesting]]
  - [[methods/contribution-analysis|methods/contribution-analysis]]
  - [[concepts/aggregation-vs-attribution|concepts/aggregation-vs-attribution]]
- **Reflection question:** For your portfolio, where is attribution genuinely
  impossible, and what would make a *contribution* claim credible to a skeptical
  funder? What evidence would change your confidence?
- **Practical exercise:** Pick one cluster of your portfolio. Sketch (a) an
  **Outcome Harvesting** plan (harvesting questions + how you'd substantiate) and
  (b) a **contribution story** outline with explicit assumptions and at least one
  rival explanation. Read [[case-studies/T0029-brush-strokes-to-bold-strokes|T0029]]
  for how Integrity combines these.

---

## Week 4 — Methods II: judgement, value & coverage

- **Learning objective:** Make defensible evaluative judgements across
  heterogeneous interventions; judge value; map coverage and gaps. *(Q1, Q3)*
- **Study (concepts/methods):**
  - [[methods/rubrics|methods/rubrics]] *(deep pass)*
  - [[methods/value-for-investment|methods/value-for-investment]]
  - [[methods/evidence-gap-maps|methods/evidence-gap-maps]]
  - [[concepts/defining-success-at-portfolio-scale|concepts/defining-success-at-portfolio-scale]]
- **Reflection question:** What does "success" mean for *your portfolio as a whole*
  (vs the sum of its grants)? How will you stay honest when the portfolio evidence
  is contradictory or ambiguous?
- **Practical exercise (★ key deliverable):** **Draft a portfolio-level evaluation
  rubric** — pick 3–4 criteria from your portfolio ToC and write concrete
  Excellent/Good/Adequate/Poor word-pictures for each (use
  [[templates/prompts|prompt #4]] and the
  [[templates/portfolio-evaluation-framework-template|framework template §3]]).
  Pilot it on two real-ish cases and check they'd be rated consistently.

---

## Week 5 — Synthesis: pulling many cases into one portfolio picture

- **Learning objective:** Synthesise across heterogeneous cases into portfolio-level
  patterns and a defensible aggregate picture. *(Q2)*
- **Study (concepts/methods):**
  - [[methods/appreciative-inquiry|methods/appreciative-inquiry]]
  - Cross-case analysis via the
    [[templates/cross-case-analysis-matrix|cross-case analysis matrix template]]
  - [[methods/evidence-gap-maps|methods/evidence-gap-maps]] *(as a synthesis output)*
- **Reflection question:** What's your guard against the two failure modes —
  meaningless averaging vs disconnected reporting? How will you surface
  *negative/null* results, not just successes?
- **Practical exercise (★ key deliverable):** **Design a cross-case analysis
  matrix** for your portfolio (use [[templates/prompts|prompt #5]]). Define the
  columns from your ToC + questions, fill 3–4 illustrative rows, then write the
  "pattern-reading" notes: what works, for whom, in which contexts — plus the
  evidence gaps.

---

## Week 6 — Communication: making findings actionable for funders

- **Learning objective:** Communicate complex portfolio findings to non-evaluator
  decision-makers so they drive decisions. *(Q4)*
- **Study (concepts/methods):**
  - [[case-studies/T0102-icf-portfolio-communication|case-studies/T0102 — communicating complex multi-country evaluations]]
  - [[templates/funder-brief-template|templates/funder-brief-template]]
  - [[methods/value-for-investment|methods/value-for-investment]] *(as the "is it worth it?" answer funders want)*
- **Reflection question:** What is the single decision your funder most needs this
  evaluation to inform — and how would you lead with it instead of with method?
- **Practical exercise (★ key deliverable):** From a mock synthesis (reuse your
  Week 5 matrix), **draft a one-page funder brief** (use
  [[templates/prompts|prompt #7]]). Then run [[templates/prompts|prompt #9]] to
  stress-test it from a skeptical funder's perspective and revise.

---

## Week 7 — Embedding evaluation culture & standing functions

- **Learning objective:** Help an organisation *institutionalise* portfolio
  evaluation so findings are owned and used over time — not a one-off report. *(Q4)*
- **Study (concepts/methods):**
  - [[concepts/evaluation-culture|concepts/evaluation-culture]]
  - [[case-studies/T0098-building-evaluation-culture|case-studies/T0098 — building an evaluation culture]]
    & [[case-studies/T0221-greo-network-evaluation|T0221 — embedding evaluation in a network]]
  - [[orgs-and-people/abt-global|orgs-and-people/abt-global]] (PMEL Nepal standing
    function) + the [[external-sources/external-sources-index|Sarah Dickins learning-systems piece]]
- **Reflection question:** What would it take for your funder to run a *standing*
  portfolio MEL function (à la Abt PMEL Nepal) rather than commissioning one-off
  evaluations? What are the first two things you'd put in place?
- **Practical exercise:** Sketch a **standing portfolio MEL / learning system** for
  your funder: the learning forums and cadence (annual portfolio review +
  operational action reviews), the shared-measurement infrastructure (a P-Dash /
  dashboard equivalent), and who owns/uses what. Use
  [[templates/prompts|prompt #10]] if it's an incubator cohort.

---

## Week 8 — Capstone: a full framework, and AI in the workflow

- **Learning objective:** Integrate everything into one coherent portfolio
  evaluation framework, and use AI responsibly to accelerate (not replace) the
  work. *(Q1–Q4)*
- **Study (concepts/methods):**
  - [[case-studies/A0248-A0249-ai-in-evaluation-masterclasses|case-studies/A0248–A0249 — AI-in-evaluation masterclasses]]
    & [[case-studies/T0208-outcome-harvesting-ai-interviewers|T0208 — Outcome Harvesting with AI interviewers]]
  - [[templates/prompts|templates/prompts]] (the full prompt set)
  - [[templates/portfolio-evaluation-framework-template|templates/portfolio-evaluation-framework-template]] (assemble the whole thing)
- **Reflection question:** Where in your framework is AI assistance genuinely safe
  and useful, and where must a human evaluator keep the judgement? How would you
  explain that line to a funder?
- **Practical exercise (★ capstone):** Produce a **complete portfolio evaluation
  framework** for your chosen funder/incubator by assembling your Weeks 2–7 outputs
  into the [[templates/portfolio-evaluation-framework-template|framework template]].
  Use [[templates/prompts|prompt #1]] to draft, then critique it yourself against
  the four open questions and the [[external-sources/norad-portfolio-mel-guide-summary#6. Cross-reference against the conference case studies|Norad cross-reference table]].

## What you can do by the end

- [ ] Define portfolio evaluation and explain why funders/incubators need it.
- [ ] Write a portfolio theory of change and two-level evaluation questions.
- [ ] Choose and justify a fit-for-purpose "bricolage" of methods.
- [ ] Build a rubric that judges heterogeneous interventions credibly.
- [ ] Run a cross-case synthesis and a contribution story with honest confidence.
- [ ] Map portfolio coverage and evidence gaps.
- [ ] Communicate findings to a non-evaluator funder on one page.
- [ ] Advise on embedding a standing portfolio MEL/learning function.
- [ ] Use Claude to accelerate the work while keeping judgement human.

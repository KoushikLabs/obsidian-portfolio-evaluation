# Overview

## Purpose

This wiki is a self-study and reference base for **portfolio-level evaluation** —
the practice of assessing a *set* of diverse interventions at an *aggregate*
level. It is aimed at people who **advise funders and incubators** on evaluating
their grant portfolios and accelerator cohorts.

## What portfolio-level evaluation is

Project evaluation asks *"did this intervention work?"* Portfolio evaluation asks
a different question:

> *"Across this whole set of diverse interventions, what is the funder
> contributing, what is working and why, where are the gaps, and what should we
> do differently — said credibly and usefully at the aggregate level?"*

A funder's body of grants, or an incubator's cohort, behaves like a **portfolio**:
a collection of heterogeneous "bets" that share a strategic intent but differ in
activities, contexts, maturity, timescales, and definitions of success. The
[[external-sources/norad-portfolio-mel-guide-summary|Norad guide]] puts it
crisply: *"a portfolio is a collection of measures … designed to contribute to
achieving higher-level objectives and … based on a common underlying logic."*

## The central tension

> **How do you say something credible and useful at the aggregate level when the
> underlying grants or ventures are genuinely heterogeneous?**

The two failure modes to avoid are **meaningless averaging** (forcing
incomparable things onto one scale) and **disconnected reporting** (a pile of
project reports with no portfolio story). See
[[concepts/the-heterogeneity-problem|the heterogeneity problem]].

## The four open questions

1. **Setting criteria across heterogeneous interventions.** When does a shared
   framework help, and when does it impose ill-suited criteria? The
   [[case-studies/T0302-portfoli-omg|Portfoli-OMG!]] session asks whether OECD-DAC
   even fits at portfolio scale; the [[external-sources/norad-portfolio-mel-guide-summary|Norad guide]]
   quietly answers "use strategic/operational questions instead", with
   [[methods/rubrics|rubrics]] to judge unlike things against shared standards.
2. **Synthesising across cases.** [[methods/outcome-harvesting|Outcome Harvesting]],
   [[methods/contribution-analysis|Contribution Analysis]], cross-case analysis,
   and [[methods/evidence-gap-maps|Evidence Gap Maps]] are the synthesis toolkit.
3. **Defining success and credibility at scale.** Success for a *portfolio* is not
   the sum of project successes; credibility means
   [[concepts/aggregation-vs-attribution|contribution, not attribution]], with
   honest confidence levels.
4. **Making findings actionable for funders.** Communicating to non-evaluator
   decision-makers ([[case-studies/T0102-icf-portfolio-communication|the ICF
   session]]) and embedding an [[concepts/evaluation-culture|evaluation culture]]
   so findings get used.

## Key themes across the case studies

1. **Bricolage of methods.** The strongest portfolio evaluations combine methods
   rather than relying on one — [[case-studies/T0029-brush-strokes-to-bold-strokes|Integrity's T0029]]
   blends Outcome Harvesting + Contribution Analysis + Appreciative Inquiry +
   rubrics + cross-case analysis.
2. **Contribution, not attribution.** The realistic causal standard at portfolio
   scale, shared by the conference work and the Norad/Itad guidance.
3. **Judgement via rubrics.** When portfolio evidence is contradictory or
   ambiguous, [[methods/rubrics|rubrics]] make the necessary judgement robust and
   auditable. The [[external-sources/itad-portfolio-mel-brief-summary|Itad framing]]
   adds: *"analytical judgements give richer, more robust insights … than simply
   aggregating numbers."*
4. **Communicating to funders.** Turning a complex multi-grant picture into a
   decision a board can act on — the "last mile" of portfolio evaluation.
5. **Embedding evaluation culture.** From a one-off study to a standing capability
   — see [[orgs-and-people/abt-global|Abt's PMEL Nepal]] standing function as the
   live instance of the Norad blueprint.

## Working thesis

*Portfolio evaluation is less about a single method than about disciplined
judgement: a clear portfolio theory of change, fit-for-purpose methods chosen as a
bricolage, contribution claims made with honest confidence, and findings
communicated so funders actually act on them.*

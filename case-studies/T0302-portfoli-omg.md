## The portfolio being evaluated

**Mega-portfolios** — portfolios so large they contain *sub-portfolios,
programmes, and projects* nested inside them. The session reflects on delivering
**Portfolio Monitoring, Evaluation and Learning (MEL)** programmes across this
kind of multi-layered structure, spanning many different interventions and
actors.

## Methods used

A portfolio MEL programme operating across the nested hierarchy
(portfolio → sub-portfolio → programme → project). The session is reflective —
about *delivering* portfolio MEL — rather than a single named method; it engages
with the question of **whether to apply an OECD-DAC framework at portfolio
scale**, and with defining evidence, success, and credibility across a portfolio.
*(Specific method mix not fully confirmed from the programme page.)*

## Key challenge addressed

The exact problems a funder faces, named directly:

- **Ill-suited criteria** — standard evaluation criteria often don't fit at
  portfolio level.
- **Evaluating at a high level of abstraction** across many different
  interventions and actors.
- **Whether to apply an OECD-DAC framework at portfolio scale** — and what to do
  when it doesn't fit.
- **How to define evidence, success, and credibility** across a whole portfolio.

This is the **core open-question set** of this knowledge base, stated almost
verbatim (see the [[index|README]]).

## Relevance to a funder / incubator

Maximum. A funder's grant portfolio and an incubator's nested programmes behave
exactly like the mega-portfolios described here. Anyone advising on portfolio
evaluation should treat this as the **reference framing** for the problem:
ill-suited criteria, abstraction across heterogeneous interventions, and the
struggle to define success/credibility at scale.

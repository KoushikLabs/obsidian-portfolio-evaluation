> **Context finding:** the parent evaluation found that portfolio MEL was *"not
> yet in place"* at Norad. The brief therefore proposes an **integrated approach**
> to build it — making this a candid funder-side blueprint.

## 1. Definition of portfolio MEL used

> **Portfolio:** *"A portfolio is a collection of measures which are designed to
> contribute to achieving higher level objectives and are based on a common
> underlying logic."*

> **Portfolio-level MEL:** *"Portfolio-level MEL ... involves gathering evidence of
> results across the range of a portfolio's interventions, and conducting a
> higher-level analysis of the extent to which the broader outcomes and objectives
> of a portfolio are being achieved."*

It is explicitly distinguished from project MEL (*"different to monitoring or
evaluating specific interventions in specific contexts"*) and framed as a
**management instrument**: MEL *"should serve portfolio management and feed
directly into decision making"* and be *"integrated into the portfolio management
cycle and tailored to fit Norad's existing systems and touchpoints for decision
making."*

---

## 2. Recommended portfolio-level evaluation questions

The guide does **not** prescribe a fixed question bank. It tells teams to derive
questions from the portfolio theory of change, recorded in a **Knowledge Plan**,
split into two types — each with **formative** and **summative** purposes. The
brief's own example matrix:

| | **Formative** (how to improve) | **Summative** (what's been achieved) |
|---|---|---|
| **Operational** (shorter-term, managing the work) | • How can we better support grantees to make progress? • How can we better spend resources across the portfolio? • How can we better support linkages, and with whom (coordination, collaboration)? • How can we improve learning processes across the portfolio? | • What has been the progress so far (activities, spend, timeframes)? • What has been achieved or is emerging (outputs, early outcomes)? • To what extent have participants been reached as intended? • What sense do we make of the portfolio's progress towards its overall aims? |
| **Strategic** (direction, contribution, big questions) | • Are we supporting the right partners? • Where else could/should we intervene in the system (strategic entry points)? • What do we now know about the enablers/barriers to progress? • Who else should we coordinate/collaborate with? • How can we better achieve synergies across the portfolio? | • What difference has been achieved (outcomes, impacts)? • For whom, in what ways, and under what circumstances? • What is the cumulative effect (across the portfolio)? • To what extent are these due to Norad's partnerships? What other factors were important? |

**Strategic questions** are set early, hold for the portfolio's duration, drive
annual reviews, and form the basis of portfolio-wide evaluations. **Operational
questions** are reviewed more frequently (team meetings, new calls for proposals,
partnership renewals).

## 3. How it handles aggregating across diverse interventions

This is the guide's most useful contribution to the heterogeneity problem.

- **Reject standardised indicators; use "buckets."** *"It's challenging to use
  standardised indicators effectively, since contexts and interventions within a
  portfolio are typically too varied."* Instead, collate evidence into **"buckets"
  of indicators** — a bucket *"groups together indicators that, while varied,
  relate to a similar theme,"* letting you sort knowledge by relevance to
  portfolio outcomes and progress markers.
- **Periodic judgement, not arithmetic.** *"You then periodically review all the
  evidence collected and make a judgement about the progress being made.
  ... at portfolio level, the evidence may be contradictory or ambiguous, and ...
  assessing progress will therefore require a judgement."*
- **Rubrics make the judgement robust.** *"Rubrics can be a useful way of
  structuring these judgements, and ensuring they are robust"* (rubric defined per
  Dart et al. 1998; Davidson 2004 — see [[methods/rubrics|methods/rubrics]]).
- **A two-tier evidence architecture** (Figure 2 of the brief):
  - *Annually:* synthesise partner reporting + evaluations + other knowledge
    (using the **P-Dash** portfolio data tool) to judge progress against the
    portfolio ToC outcomes.
  - *Every 3–4 years (minimum):* a **whole-portfolio evaluation** that synthesises
    monitoring data + additional primary data to judge **overall results and
    contribution** and answer strategic questions.
- **Contribution, not attribution.** Whole-portfolio evaluations make *"judgement
  on overall portfolio results and contribution."* The guide is candid that even
  contribution analysis *"will often not be possible"* where funding flows to
  multilaterals or as core funding.
- **Broad definition of knowledge** (like Luminate and Mastercard Foundation):
  **professional, operational, technical, and contextual** knowledge — not just
  formal evaluation evidence.

## 4. Frameworks / criteria recommended

- **Portfolio-level theory of change** — the primary framework and the integrating
  spine. The brief contrasts intervention-level vs portfolio-level ToC:
  *specific → systemic; narrow → broad; less complex → more complex; detailed →
  conceptual; operational → strategic.* It is **systems-oriented** (specify
  Norad's contribution, system entry points, how the system shapes change) and
  supports **nested theories of change** (Mayne 2015) for multi-country portfolios.
- **Theory-based evaluation** for whole-portfolio evaluations (Chen 2012; Coryn et
  al. 2011; Funnell & Rogers 2011) — analyse the ToC, review monitoring evidence,
  take country/thematic case-study deep dives, judge *"what works, for whom, in
  which contexts."*
- **Rubrics** (Dart et al. 1998; Davidson 2004) to structure judgements.
- **Simple learning frameworks** for sense-making: "what worked well/less well /
  do differently"; **SWOT**; and an "expected vs actual / what did we learn"
  agreed-facts-vs-shared-opinions frame.
- **Adaptive, iterative management** — MEL feeds the management cycle, not a
  parallel compliance process.
- ❗ **OECD-DAC criteria are NOT used.** The brief organises evaluation around
  strategic/operational questions and contribution judgements, *not* the DAC
  relevance/effectiveness/efficiency/impact/sustainability criteria. (Some
  summative questions echo DAC concerns — "what difference," "for whom under what
  circumstances" — but the DAC framework is never invoked.)

---

## 5. The five-step model (the spine of the guide)

1. **Develop a portfolio-level theory of change** — systemic, high-level; sets
   direction and situates combined contributions within the broader system.
2. **Define strategic & operational questions + a Knowledge Plan** — aligned to the
   ToC; drive what MEL needs to answer.
3. **Establish monitoring processes** — progress markers + "buckets" of indicators
   + P-Dash; build the evidence bank; plan space for sense-making.
4. **Plan for evaluations** — whole-portfolio evaluations (every 3–4 years min.) +
   smaller periodic/focused evaluations; theory-based; with financing options.
5. **Establish spaces for learning** — collaborative reflection and sense-making
   (annual portfolio review for strategic questions; action reviews for
   operational ones) so judgements about direction and progress get made and used.

## 6. Cross-reference against the conference case studies

Where the Norad/Itad guide **agrees** and **differs** with the
[[case-studies/case-studies-index|case studies]]:

| Theme | Norad/Itad guide | Conference sessions | Agree / differ |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Heterogeneity** | Standardised indicators don't work; use "buckets" + judgement | [[case-studies/T0302-portfoli-omg\|T0302]] names "ill-suited criteria" and "high level of abstraction" as the core problem | **Strong agreement** on the problem; Norad offers a concrete coping mechanism (buckets + rubrics) |
| **OECD-DAC at portfolio scale** | DAC criteria **not used**; organises around strategic/operational questions | [[case-studies/T0302-portfoli-omg\|T0302]] explicitly debates *whether* to apply OECD-DAC at portfolio scale | **Tension/divergence** — Norad effectively answers "don't"; the conference treats it as an open question |
| **Contribution vs attribution** | Contribution language; attribution "often not possible" | [[case-studies/T0029-brush-strokes-to-bold-strokes\|T0029]] builds contribution via Contribution Analysis + Outcome Harvesting | **Agreement** on contribution as the standard |
| **Rubrics** | Explicitly recommended to make judgements robust | [[case-studies/T0029-brush-strokes-to-bold-strokes\|T0029]] uses rubrics + quality-of-evidence assessment | **Agreement** |
| **Named methods** | Theory-based evaluation, monitoring, rubrics; **does not name** Outcome Harvesting / Appreciative Inquiry | [[case-studies/T0029-brush-strokes-to-bold-strokes\|T0029]] adds Outcome Harvesting + Appreciative Inquiry ("bricolage") | **Difference in method palette** — the conference is richer/more eclectic; Norad is leaner |
| **Synthesis route** | Whole-portfolio theory-based evaluation + country/thematic case studies | [[case-studies/T0029-brush-strokes-to-bold-strokes\|T0029]] cross-case analysis; [[case-studies/T0102-icf-portfolio-communication\|T0102]] multi-country synthesis & communication | **Agreement** (case-study deep dives ≈ cross-case analysis); conference adds the *communication* emphasis Norad underplays |
| **Embedding / learning culture** | Step 5 "spaces for learning"; MEL serves management | [[case-studies/T0098-building-evaluation-culture\|T0098]] (build evaluation culture); [[case-studies/T0221-greo-network-evaluation\|T0221]] (dashboards) | **Strong agreement**; Norad's P-Dash ≈ Greo's dashboards |
| **Standing function vs one-off** | Proposes an ongoing integrated MEL approach (but Norad hadn't built it yet) | [[orgs-and-people/abt-global\|Abt PMEL Nepal]] is a working standing function | **Agreement** — Abt is the live instance of Norad's blueprint |

**Net:** the Norad/Itad guide and the conference cases **agree on the core
diagnosis** (heterogeneity, contribution-not-attribution, judgement via rubrics,
embedding learning). They **differ** in two ways: (1) Norad quietly *resolves* the
OECD-DAC question by not using DAC criteria, whereas T0302 keeps it open; and (2)
the conference work (especially Integrity's bricolage) uses a **wider method
palette** (Outcome Harvesting, Appreciative Inquiry) than the leaner Norad guide,
and puts more weight on **communicating** complex findings (T0102).
